### 01-hook.mp3  (✅ already done, no need to record it)
Thirteen people. Trapped four kilometers inside a mountain.

The water is rising. The air is running out.

And outside, nobody even knows if they're alive.

This is the true story of eighteen days in the dark... and the rescue the whole world was watching.

### 02-the-adventure.mp3
June twenty-third, twenty eighteen. Northern Thailand.

Twelve boys from a football team called the Wild Boars finish practice with their twenty-five-year-old coach, Ek.

It's a teammate's birthday, so they decide to celebrate with a little adventure: exploring Tham Luang, a giant cave under the mountains.

They've been inside before. They leave their bikes at the entrance, grab some snacks and flashlights, and walk into the dark.

The plan is simple: one hour, then home.

### 03-the-flood.mp3
But while they're deep inside, the sky outside opens up.

The monsoon season has arrived early. Rainwater pours into the mountain, and the cave begins to flood.

Water rushes into the tunnels behind them, faster than they can walk.

The way out is gone.

So they do the only thing they can. They go deeper, climbing higher and higher, until they reach a muddy ledge, four kilometers from the entrance.

There, in total darkness, they wait.

### 04-outside.mp3
That night, parents start to worry. The boys haven't come home.

Someone finds the bikes at the cave entrance... and the cave is full of water.

By morning, rescuers arrive. But every tunnel is flooded, and the rain won't stop.

### 05-in-the-dark.mp3
Inside, the hours turn into days.

They have no food left. Their flashlights are dying. It is cold, and the air is thick and still.

But their coach, Ek, knows how to stay calm. He used to be a Buddhist monk.

He teaches the boys to meditate: to breathe slowly, and save their energy.

They drink the clean water dripping from the cave walls. They take turns digging into the rock, hoping to find a way out.

And they stay together.

Here is the science: staying calm really matters. Panic makes your heart race and burns energy faster. Slow breathing uses less oxygen... and in a cave, oxygen is everything.

### 06-the-world-helps.mp3
Outside, the story spreads around the world.

Thai Navy SEALs arrive. Then cave divers from Britain, Australia, the United States, China, Japan, and many more.

More than ten thousand people come to help.

Giant pumps pull water out of the cave, more than a billion liters. Volunteers search the mountain for hidden openings. Local farmers even let the water flood their rice fields, ruining their own crops to help.

Days go by. Five... seven... nine. And still, no sign of the boys.

### 07-found.mp3
Then, on day ten, two British divers push further than anyone has gone before.

Swimming through freezing, muddy water so dark they can barely see their own hands...

they surface in a chamber... and smell something.

People.

Around the world, millions of people cheer.

### 08-the-problem.mp3
But there's a huge problem.

Finding them was one thing. Getting them out is another.

The way out is a journey of hours through flooded tunnels, some so narrow that a diver has to take off his air tank to squeeze through.

Most of the boys have never dived before. Some of them can't even swim.

And waiting is not an option. More heavy rain is coming, and the oxygen in their chamber is dropping. Normal air has twenty-one percent oxygen. Here, it is falling toward fifteen.

At that level, every breath gets harder.

### 09-a-hero.mp3
Then comes the saddest moment of the whole story.

Saman Kunan, a former Navy SEAL who volunteered to help, is carrying air tanks to the boys.

On his way back, he runs out of air himself... and he doesn't make it.

He gave his life so that thirteen others could live. Today, he is remembered as a hero.

### 10-the-plan.mp3
Now everyone knows: there is no more time.

The rescuers come up with a plan that has never been tried before.

Each boy will get a full-face mask, a wetsuit, and medicine from a doctor to keep him calm and asleep, so he won't panic underwater.

Then, one by one, each boy will be carried by a diver, following a guide rope, all the way through the flooded cave.

Along the route, more than a hundred divers and helpers wait at stations with spare air.

It's risky. It's never been done. But it's their only chance.

### 11-the-rescue.mp3
Day sixteen. The rescue begins.

Hours later, the first boy reaches the entrance, alive. Then another. By night, four boys are out.

Day seventeen: four more.

Day eighteen: the last four boys... and finally, Coach Ek.

Thirteen out of thirteen.

And just after the last divers come out, the main water pumps fail, and the water rushes back in.

### 12-the-end.mp3
Eighteen days in the dark. Thousands of people from all over the world, working as one team.

The boys recovered in hospital, went back to school, and back to football.

Their story reminds us of three things: stay calm, stay together... and never give up.
